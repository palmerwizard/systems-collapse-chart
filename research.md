# Systems Collapse: Long-Run Trajectories of Thirteen US Institutional Systems, 1970-2050

## Research Report

Prepared October 2026. This document combines two research runs: long-run decline and hollowing trajectories for thirteen US institutional systems, and automation penetration trajectories for the same thirteen systems. Figures were checked against primary sources in October 2026, and corrections from that check are applied throughout. Where a figure could not be verified, the text says so.

## Scope and Methods

The thirteen systems covered are: (1) Psychological and mental-health care, (2) Financial system, (3) Law and legal system, (4) Healthcare, (5) Education, (6) Media and information, (7) Housing, (8) Labor and work, (9) Food system, (10) Community and social fabric, (11) Governance and democracy, (12) Energy, treated here as emotional energy and attention (burnout, exhaustion) and, in the automation section, as the power grid (metering), and (13) Entertainment.

The guiding frame, that these systems increasingly run on symbols (grades, scores, codes, counts, credentials) while the underlying function hollows out, is the requesting party's framing, not a consensus finding in the sources. What the sources document, system by system, is a consistent set of measurable divergences: inputs, prices, counts, credentials, and reported activity rise or stay high while outcome measures, per-capita participation, and public confidence fall. That pattern is strong for media, housing, higher education, finance, labor organization, and community life; mixed or partially reversing for healthcare outcomes, incarceration, suicide, and overdoses in 2023-2024; and contested on magnitude for AI displacement, housing-shortage size, and attention span.

Sources preferred are primary or reputable: Gallup, Pew Research Center, the Bureau of Labor Statistics, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services, the Congressional Budget Office, the OECD, academic studies, and major industry reports. Each claim carries its source inline. Evidence flagged thin or contested in the research is flagged thin or contested here rather than presented as consensus.

Confidence key used throughout: High means official statistics, a large representative survey, or a primary regulator. Medium means a reputable analyst survey (World Economic Forum, McKinsey, Thomson Reuters, American Medical Association, Gallup). Thin means a commercial market report or aggregator only, flagged in place.

Four standing caveats apply to the whole document:

1. No credible 2040 or 2050 per-system forecast exists for any individual system. The only defensible long-horizon anchor is cross-cutting: McKinsey Global Institute, that 50 percent of current work activities could be automated between 2030 and 2060, midpoint 2045. Any per-system 2040 or 2050 point in a plot is an extrapolation by the plotter and must be drawn dashed and labeled as such.
2. World Economic Forum and McKinsey figures are employer estimates, scenario ranges, and technical ceilings, not measured outcomes. Exposure and technical potential are not forecasts of job loss or adoption.
3. Union-density figures for 2025 come from a Bureau of Labor Statistics release that flags the 2025 Current Population Survey as not strictly comparable with prior years (October 2025 was not collected during the federal shutdown; figures use 11-month averages).
4. The 1984 commercial-bank count carries a 13-bank vintage difference (14,483 versus 14,496) depending on series definition. Both figures are shown as a range.

A note on the integrity index: no integrity index appears in either underlying research report. The reports label their trajectory classifications as author synthesis and warn repeatedly that no credible 2040 or 2050 per-system forecast exists. Any integrity index shown in a chart or artifact built from this research is an artifact-layer construct, a synthesized comparative shape with no basis in the cited research, and must be labeled as synthesized, not as a measured series.

## Cross-System Trust: The Single Best Series

Gallup Confidence in Institutions, average "great deal / quite a lot" confidence across 14 core institutions, is the strongest cross-system series in the set. It averaged 40-50 percent in the 1980s-1990s, fell to a record low of 26 percent in 2023, measured 28 percent in 2025 (July 17, 2025 release, the fourth consecutive year below 30 percent), and 27 percent in 2026 (July 14, 2026 release, the fifth consecutive year below 30 percent).

Per-institution confidence in the July 2026 release (survey June 1-15, 2026): Congress 9 percent, television news 14 percent, newspapers, big business, and the criminal justice system 17 percent each, presidency 27 percent, Supreme Court 27 percent, public schools 27 percent, medical system 28 percent, banks 28 percent, church 33 percent, police 45 percent, military 61 percent, small business 67 percent.

Pew Research Center trust in the federal government ("always" or "most of the time"): 73 percent in 1958, 22 percent in 2024, 17 percent in 2025 (2 percent "just about always" plus 15 percent "most of the time"), near the lowest in about 70 years. Democrats fell to 9 percent in 2025, their record low; Republicans measured 26 percent.

Edelman Trust Barometer (average trust in business, government, media, and NGOs): US Trust Index 47 in both 2025 and 2026, among the five lowest of large economies, against a global 28-country average of 56-57 and China at 77-80.

Trajectory classification (author synthesis of the sources below):

- Accelerating or continued decline: local media, housing affordability, community and social connection, manager and employee engagement, federal debt, health-spending share.
- Decline with plateau at a low level: trust in government, courts, and banks; union density; theatrical attendance; labor-force participation.
- Partial repair or recent reversal, not to be over-claimed as collapse: life expectancy and overdose deaths (2024 record improvement), incarceration (down from the 2008 peak, ticking up since 2020), suicide (small 2024 dip), frequent mental distress in young adults (down 2022-2024 but far above 2012), higher-education confidence (36 percent in 2024 rising to 42 percent in 2025).

## System 1: Psychological and Mental-Health Care

Symbol versus function: diagnoses, prescriptions, therapy visits, apps, and crisis-line counts expand, while population outcomes (suicide, distress, severe-illness care capacity) did not improve in parallel for two decades.

### Anchors

- 1963-1980, deinstitutionalization without the promised community system. Community Mental Health Act 1963. State psychiatric census about 558,000 in 1955, about 200,000 by the mid-1970s, under 40,000 today; beds about 237 per 100,000 in 1970 falling to 37 per 100,000 in 2020 (Kaiser Family Foundation figure cited in secondary synthesis). Jails, emergency rooms, and streets became de facto providers, a claim made by critics and disputed in part by Mad in America (2023), which notes that total (private plus public) beds tell a less simple story. The magnitude of state bed loss is not contested; its causal weight for homelessness and incarceration is.
- 1980, DSM-III shifts psychiatry to checklist diagnosis; 1987, Prozac approved; 1997, the Food and Drug Administration loosens direct-to-consumer drug advertising (the United States is one of only two high-income countries allowing it). Prescribing becomes the scalable, reimbursable unit.
- 1999-2020, more treatment, worse headline outcome. Both antidepressant prescription prevalence and the suicide rate rose; ecological analyses find a positive, not negative, association at population level (American Psychiatric Association, *Crisis* study; CDC WONDER and the Medical Expenditure Panel Survey). This does not prove antidepressants cause suicide; the authors stress ecological limits.
- 2008, Mental Health Parity Act; 2010, Affordable Care Act expands coverage on paper; workforce shortages and out-of-network care persist (widely reported; no single number in the research set).
- 2022, 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline launched.

### Trust and legitimacy

No long Gallup series on the mental-health system exists. Proxy: Gallup medical system confidence, 80 percent in 1975, 34 percent in 2023, 28 percent in the June 2026 survey. Help-seeking stigma fell (utilization rose) even as system confidence fell. Legitimacy is split: therapy as personal practice is normalized, while the system is distrusted for access and cost.

### Current state, 2024-2026

- Suicide: the rate rose 37 percent from 2000 to 2018, reached 14.2 per 100,000 in 2022 (final data, National Center for Health Statistics Data Brief 572; the highest since 1941), then 14.1 in 2023 and 13.7 in 2024 (48,824 deaths), a small dip the CDC and Associated Press describe as possibly a blip.
- Frequent mental distress, ages 18-29 (CDC Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System): 12.7 percent in 2012, 23.7 percent in 2022, 21.3 percent in 2024, improving for two years but still 8.6 points above 2012, with 47 of 53 jurisdictions up over the period.
- Antidepressant use: 11.4 percent of US adults in 2023 (CDC), cited in 2026 secondary reporting; adolescent dispensing accelerated 63.5 percent after pandemic onset in one cited study, to be verified against the primary paper before reuse in a graphic.

### Automation penetration

Among US adolescents and young adults aged 12-21, 13.1 percent used AI chatbots for mental-health advice in the 2024 survey wave, rising to 19.2 percent in 2025, a rise of about 50 percent in a year; 42.8 percent of users did so at least monthly, and 91.7 percent rated it helpful (RAND, Brown, and Harvard, nationally representative, n about 1,009-1,058; published in *JAMA Network Open* for the 2024 wave and *JAMA Pediatrics* for the 2025 wave). Use among 18-21-year-olds was higher, about 22-24 percent. No representative US figure exists for the share of therapy sessions delivered by AI. Commercial aggregator claims (for example, "34 percent of US adults used AI therapy tools by 2024") are thin and unverified and must not be plotted. No credible per-system penetration forecast for 2030, 2040, or 2050 exists. The only dated forecasts are revenue: AI mental-health chatbots 1.46 billion dollars (2024) rising to 10.16 billion dollars (2034), compound annual growth rate 21.3 percent (Precedence Research); Fortune Business Insights puts AI-powered mental-health chatbots at 0.57 billion dollars (2025) rising to 7.8 billion dollars (2034), compound annual growth rate 33.6 percent. The 12-point growth-rate disagreement between vendors shows why revenue cannot be converted to a penetration percentage. Plot points: (2024, 13.1 percent; 2025, 19.2 percent, RAND and co-authors). No credible forecast points for 2030, 2040, or 2050.

### Forward trajectory

Plateau at high distress, with workforce and AI contested. No credible official 2050 mental-health prevalence forecast was found. Directional drivers cited across sources: teletherapy and AI chatbots scale contacts fastest (lowest cost per symbol), psychiatrist and psychologist supply grows slowly (Health Resources and Services Administration and Bureau of Labor Statistics projections are modest), and 988 and screening may be contributing to the 2024 suicide dip. Trajectory: partial repair possible on suicide, accelerating substitution on delivery (human to app and AI). Flag as thin evidence; do not put a precise percentage on AI-therapy displacement.

Timeline: 1963 Community Mental Health Act, 1970s bed collapse, 1980 DSM-III, 1987 Prozac, 1997 direct-to-consumer ads, 2000-2018 suicide up 37 percent, 2022 launch of 988 and suicide peak 14.2 per 100,000, 2024 dip to 13.7 per 100,000.

## System 2: Financial System

Symbol versus function: assets, trading volume, profits, and credit scores multiply; intermediation to productive enterprise and local lending shrinks as a share, and crises socialize losses.

### Anchors

- 1971, Nixon ends gold convertibility; 1978-1982, deregulation begins (deposit-rate ceilings phased out, Depository Institutions Deregulation and Monetary Control Act 1980, Garn-St Germain 1982). Finance was about 3-3.5 percent of GDP in the 1970s.
- 1987 crash; 1994, Riegle-Neal interstate banking; 1999, Gramm-Leach-Bliley repeals Glass-Steagall separation. Banks: 14,483-14,496 commercial banks in 1984 (range reflects series-definition vintage), 7,061 in 2008, 4,027 in 2023 (about 4,400-4,500 FDIC-insured including thrifts in 2024), a fall of about 72 percent (Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation data via University of Illinois farmdoc daily).
- By 2007 finance was 5.9 percent of GDP. Financial profits grew about 800 percent in real terms from 1980 to 2005, against about 250 percent for non-financial profits; the finance share of corporate profits rose from about 15 percent (1980) to about 30 percent (2016, briefly about 50 percent in the early 2000s in some series) (financialization literature summarized in the financialization entry and Mason and co-authors).
- 2008, Global Financial Crisis: bailouts, zero rates, quantitative easing. The Federal Reserve balance sheet relative to GDP went from about 6-7 percent pre-2008 to about 20 percent in 2019 and about 35 percent in 2020 in one cited series.
- 2023, Silicon Valley Bank, Signature, and First Republic failures, the largest since 2008; consolidation continued.

### Trust

Gallup banks: 60 percent in 1979, 26 percent in the 2022 summary, 30 percent in the 2024 poll, 28 percent in the 2026 release. Big business: 34 percent (1975), 14 percent (2022 low), 17-25 percent (2024-2025, question-wording dependent). Trust never recovered to pre-2008 levels in the Gallup trend.

### Current state, 2024-2026

- Federal debt held by the public reached about 100 percent of GDP at end-2025 (about 31.3 trillion dollars) in Treasury and FRED reporting cited by Forbes. The finance and government boundary is now the story.
- Household debt (Federal Reserve Bank of New York, widely reported, about 18 trillion dollars in 2024-2025). Use the New York Fed primary release for the exact quarter before publishing a graphic; not re-verified live in the research run.

### Automation penetration

Algorithmic trading is consistently estimated at about 70-73 percent of US equity trading volume in 2024. One market report attributes approximately 73 percent to Goldman Sachs (2024); another attributes 73 percent of US equity trades in 2024 to the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority. Estimates for major equity and foreign-exchange markets generally cluster at 60-75 percent, foreign exchange above 90 percent in some estimates. Flag: exchanges and the Securities and Exchange Commission do not publish an official algorithmic-share series; treat 70-73 percent as an analyst consensus range, not an official statistic. There is little headroom; this curve is essentially flat and saturated to 2050 barring a definition change. Banking operations: Citi GPS, *AI in Finance: Bot, Bank and Beyond* (June 2024): 54 percent of banking jobs have high automation potential, the highest of any sector, plus 12 percent with high augmentation potential; Citi projected AI could lift the global banking profit pool about 9 percent (about 170 billion dollars) by 2028. Bloomberg Intelligence (January 2025, survey of 93 bank chief information and chief technology officers): banks expect a net workforce cut of about 3 percent over 3-5 years, 25 percent of respondents expecting 5-10 percent; up to 200,000 global bank jobs at risk, concentrated in back and middle office and customer service. Adoption proxy: about 78 percent of banks and organizations report AI deployed in at least one function (McKinsey Global Survey, as summarized in industry coverage), which is adoption breadth, not share of operations automated. The often-quoted "70 percent of basic financial operations automated by 2025, 80 percent by 2030" traces to low-quality secondary and SSRN summaries, thin, and must not be plotted as fact. Plot points: (2024, 70-73 percent algorithmic share of US equity volume, Goldman Sachs and FINRA as cited by market reports, medium and thin); (2024, 54 percent banking jobs with high automation potential, Citi GPS, ceiling not penetration); (2028, plus 9 percent banking profit pool from AI, Citi GPS, economic forecast not penetration percentage). No credible percentage forecast for the share of banking operations automated in 2030, 2040, or 2050.

### Forward trajectory

Congressional Budget Office extended baseline: debt held by the public 107 percent of GDP in 2029 (exceeding the World War II peak) rising to 156 percent in 2055, deficits averaging 6.3 percent of GDP over 30 years; alternative (tax cuts extended and similar) about 220 percent by 2055 (Congressional Budget Office Long-Term Budget Outlook 2025-2055). AI exposure for finance is among the highest of any sector (Citi and Goldman task studies cite about 30 percent or more of banking jobs affected, secondary). Trajectory: accelerating financialization plus fiscal dominance risk, not repair, unless Congressional Budget Office assumptions change.

Timeline: 1971 fiat dollar, 1980-1982 deregulation, 1999 Glass-Steagall repeal, 2007 finance 5.9 percent of GDP, 2008 crisis and bailouts, 2023 regional-bank failures, 2025 debt about 100 percent of GDP, 2055 Congressional Budget Office projection 156 percent.

## System 3: Law and Legal System

Symbol versus function: filings, plea counts, incarceration, and legal spend as activity; the trial, the system's truth-testing ritual, has nearly vanished, and civil legal need is mostly unmet.

### Anchors

- 1970, plea-bargaining legitimation (Brady v. United States, 1970); 1973, Rockefeller drug laws; 1984, Sentencing Reform Act and guidelines; 1994, Crime Bill. Mass incarceration builds.
- Vanishing trial: federal criminal trials were about 20 percent of dispositions after World War II, about 6 percent in 2000, under 3 percent in 2010, about 2 percent today; only 2.5 percent of federal convictions in 2022 resulted from trials (American Bar Association). Civil trials in federal court: 11.5 percent in 1962 falling to about 1 percent today (Duke Judicature; American Bar Association plea-bargaining principles).
- Incarceration peak 2008: 2.31 million in prisons and jails, 760 per 100,000, 1 in 99 adults (Pew and Bureau of Justice Statistics). That is about 500-600 percent above early-1970s levels (about 330,000 in prisons in 1972 in the standard Bureau of Justice Statistics series, a background figure to verify against the Bureau table for a graphic).
- Legal aid gap: Legal Services Corporation Justice Gap studies (2017, 2022) find about 85-92 percent of low-income Americans' civil legal problems receive inadequate or no legal help, a widely cited Legal Services Corporation figure, not re-opened live in the research run, flagged for primary verification.

### Trust

Gallup criminal justice system: 17 percent in the 2026 release (14 percent low in 2022). Gallup judicial system and courts broadly: confidence 35 percent in 2024, a record low, down 24 points in four years, while the OECD median stayed majority-positive; the US-OECD gap is 20 points, the largest in the trend since 2006 (Gallup). Supreme Court: trust "great deal or fair amount" for the judicial branch was at least 60 percent in all but one year from 1972 to 2020, then under 50 percent for five straight years to 2026; Democrats 58 percent (2020) falling to 23 percent (2025-2026), Republicans 79 percent (Gallup).

### Current state, 2024-2026

Incarcerated population 1.85 million at year-end 2023 (Bureau of Justice Statistics), total correctional population about 5.6 million, below peak but rising about 2 percent from 2022 to 2024 after the COVID dip. Court confidence at a record low (above).

### Automation penetration

Thomson Reuters, *Generative AI in Professional Services* 2025: generative AI use in legal nearly doubled, 14 percent (2024) rising to 26 percent (2025); in the law-firm cut, 28 percent of firms had begun integrating it, and 59 percent of firms said it should be applied to their work. Thomson Reuters 2026 follow-up: 41 percent of law firms and 47 percent of corporate legal departments report active generative AI use (up from 28 percent and 23 percent in 2025 on that survey's basis). The two series use different questions; do not splice them into one clean line without a footnote. Potential and dated forecast (medium, dated and United Kingdom based): Deloitte (2016), *Developing Legal Talent*: about 114,000 United Kingdom legal jobs, 39 percent of the sector, have a high chance of being automated over the following 20 years, that is by about 2036; technology had already displaced about 31,000 (mostly secretarial) jobs. This is the most-cited dated legal forecast, but it is United Kingdom based, pre-generative-AI, and a risk share, not a predicted penetration. Task-level proxy (medium): McKinsey (2023): the automated share of hours for business and legal professionals rises about 14 points with generative AI to about 30 percent of hours by 2030. Goldman Sachs (2023) singled out legal and administrative work as among the most exposed occupations, without a legal-only percentage in the widely reported summary. Plot points: (2024, 14 percent, legal professionals using generative AI, Thomson Reuters 2025 report); (2025, 26 percent, Thomson Reuters 2025 report); (2026, 41 percent, law firms actively using generative AI, Thomson Reuters 2026 report, different basis); (2030, about 30 percent, business and legal hours that could be automated, McKinsey 2023, potential and adoption scenario); (2036, 39 percent, United Kingdom legal jobs at high automation risk, Deloitte 2016, ceiling, United Kingdom). No credible forecast for 2040 or 2050.

### Forward trajectory

No official legal-system 2050 forecast. Credible directional forecasts: AI document review and research hit junior-lawyer and paralegal tasks first; Goldman Sachs and International Monetary Fund cited estimates in secondary AI roundups put about 44 percent of legal tasks exposed; the plea-to-trial ratio has no reform forecast reversing it; incarceration projections (Bureau of Justice Statistics and Prison Policy Initiative) are essentially flat to slightly up. Trajectory: plateau at high incarceration and low trial rate, accelerating automation of routine legal work.

Timeline: 1970 plea system legitimated, 1984 sentencing guidelines, 2008 incarceration peak 2.31 million, 2022 trials equal 2.5 percent of federal convictions, 2024 court confidence 35 percent record low.

## System 4: Healthcare

Symbol versus function: billing codes, relative value units, quality metrics, and coverage counts; the United States spends about 2.5 times peers for below-average longevity, with a genuine 2024 outcome rebound that must be reported.

### Anchors

- 1965, Medicare and Medicaid (pre-window anchor); 1973, Health Maintenance Organization Act; 1983, Medicare prospective payment and diagnosis-related groups. Payment by code begins in earnest.
- Spending share: about 7 percent of GDP in 1970 in the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services historical series (background, verify CMS National Health Expenditure Table 1 for a graphic), 17.6 percent in 2023, 18.0 percent in 2024. Per capita (CMS via a synthesis of CMS National Health Expenditure data and Census data): 384 dollars in 1971 (4.3 percent of median household income), 4,855 dollars in 2000, 14,570 dollars in 2024 (18.1 percent).
- 2010, Affordable Care Act: the uninsured rate falls from about 16 percent (2010) to about 8 percent (about 27 million people, Commonwealth Fund 2026). Coverage improves on paper; affordability does not track.
- 2014-2021, life expectancy stalls then collapses: peaked just under 79 in 2014, flat, then 76.1-76.4 in 2021 (COVID-19 and overdoses, over 1.2 million COVID deaths).

### Trust

Gallup medical system: 80 percent (1975), 34 percent (2023), 36 percent (2024 poll), 28 percent (June 2026 survey).

### Current state, 2024-2026

- OECD *Health at a Glance 2025*: United States 14,885 dollars purchasing-power-parity per person (2024), 2.49 times the OECD average of 5,967 dollars; life expectancy 78.4 years against an 81.1 OECD average; infant mortality 5.5 against 4.0 per 1,000.
- Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services actuals: 5.3 trillion dollars in 2024, 15,474 dollars per person, 18.0 percent of GDP (Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services, reported January 2026).
- Partial repair: CDC National Center for Health Statistics, life expectancy 79.0 in 2024, the highest on record; overdose deaths down 26.2 percent to 79,384, the largest annual drop recorded; heart and cancer deaths also fell. The United States still ranks below dozens of peers.

### Automation penetration

Physician adoption (high): American Medical Association Augmented Intelligence survey (n about 1,200): physicians reporting AI use in practice 38 percent (2023) rising to 66 percent (2024). Top uses are documentation and billing codes, visit notes, and discharge instructions, that is administrative first and assistive diagnosis second. Administrative automation (high): Council for Affordable Quality Healthcare Index, the industry transaction census. Fully electronic prior authorization is the lagging transaction: 26 percent fully electronic in 2021 (39 percent partially electronic, 35 percent fully manual), rising in the 2023-2025 Council for Affordable Quality Healthcare editions to roughly 31-40 percent depending on the medical or dental cut and year (2025 edition chart: prior authorization is the lowest bar in a set where eligibility verification is 98 percent and claim submission about 96-98 percent fully electronic). The lesson for the curve: back-office healthcare automation is bimodal. Claims and eligibility are already over 90 percent electronic; prior authorization is the tail. Diagnostics (count, not percentage): Food and Drug Administration list of AI-enabled medical devices, about 950 authorized by August 2024, about 1,247 by mid-2025, of which about 76-77 percent are radiology. This measures regulatory supply, not the percentage of scans read by AI; no US figure for the latter was found from a reputable source. Potential and forecast (medium): McKinsey (2023 summaries of its healthcare work): up to about 30 percent of healthcare administrative tasks could be automated; about 10 percent of nursing activities could be freed by automation (McKinsey, *Transforming Healthcare with AI*, 2020, still widely cited). McKinsey's newer US workforce modeling (2025) expects slower realized adoption in clinical roles: about 26 percent of healthcare-professional work hours automated or augmented by 2035, against about 80 percent for office and administrative support, the single most useful dated clinical-versus-admin contrast. Plot points: (2023, 38 percent, physicians using AI, American Medical Association); (2024, 66 percent, American Medical Association); (2021, 26 percent, prior authorizations fully electronic, Council for Affordable Quality Healthcare Index 2021); (2025, about 40 percent, prior authorizations fully electronic, medical plans, Council for Affordable Quality Healthcare Index 2025, read the chart with care, series definitions shifted); (2030, about 30 percent, healthcare administrative tasks automatable, McKinsey, potential); (2035, about 26 percent, healthcare-professional hours automated or augmented, McKinsey 2025 workforce model). No credible forecast for 2040 or 2050.

### Forward trajectory

Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services actuaries (*Health Affairs* 2025): health spending grows 5.8 percent per year against GDP growth of 4.3 percent per year from 2024 to 2033, reaching 20.3 percent of GDP and 8.6 trillion dollars by 2033, even as the insured share falls after enhanced marketplace credits expire. Trajectory: cost curve accelerating, outcomes plateau or partial repair. The divergence widens even in the good-outcome scenario.

Timeline: 1973 Health Maintenance Organization Act, 1983 diagnosis-related groups, 2010 Affordable Care Act, 2014 life expectancy peak about 79, 2021 life expectancy about 76.1, 2024 spending 5.3 trillion dollars and life expectancy 79.0 record, 2033 projected 20.3 percent of GDP.

## System 5: Education

Symbol versus function: enrollment, credits, grade point averages (rising), and degrees; measured basic skills flat to falling for a decade, reading for pleasure collapsing.

### Anchors

- 1970s baseline: public four-year tuition 394 dollars per year in 1970-71 (National Center for Education Statistics); total cost of a public degree about 0.60 years of median household income in 1971.
- 1978, Middle Income Student Assistance Act expands loans to all students; 1992 and 2005-2006 loan-limit and PLUS expansions follow. Bennett-hypothesis evidence: Federal Reserve Bank of New York (2015), about 0.60 dollars tuition pass-through per 1 dollar of subsidized loan, contested magnitude.
- 1983, *A Nation at Risk*; 2001, No Child Left Behind (test-count accountability); 2010s, achievement peak. National Assessment of Educational Progress Long-Term Trend age-13 scores peaked in 2012.
- Grade inflation: high-school and college grade point averages rose steadily while National Assessment of Educational Progress and SAT scores did not (ACT and College Board research, background), a classic symbol and function split. Cite the ACT 2022 grade point average study in a final graphic if used.

### Trust

Gallup public schools: 62 percent (1976 summary), 29 percent (2024), 27 percent (2026 release). Higher education specifically: 57 percent "great deal or quite a lot" in 2015, 36 percent in 2024, 42 percent in 2025 (Gallup-Lumina), a real 2025 tick up.

### Current state, 2024-2026

- Tuition (College Board 2024-25): 11,610 dollars in-state public four-year, 43,350 dollars private nonprofit; total public cost 24,920 dollars in 2024, equal to 1.24 years of median household income (against 0.60 in 1971). Student debt 1.64 trillion dollars (second quarter 2025), about 43 million borrowers; federal portfolio about 1.7 trillion dollars and 42 million or more borrowers, average balance about 40,768 dollars in 2026 reporting.
- National Assessment of Educational Progress: grade 12 reading and math at the lowest in National Assessment of Educational Progress history in 2024 reporting; Long-Term Trend age-13 reading 256 in 2023, statistically at its 1971 level; age-13 math down 15 points from the 2012 peak by the 2025 wave, lowest performers back to 1970s levels, some below 1971. Reading for fun daily, age 13: 35 percent in 1985 falling to 14 percent in 2023-2025 (National Center for Education Statistics via Chalkbeat and The 74).

### Automation penetration

Gallup and Walton Family Foundation, *Teaching for Tomorrow* (March-April 2025, n equals 2,232 US public kindergarten-through-12 teachers): 60 percent used an AI tool for work in 2024-25; 32 percent at least weekly. By task (at least monthly): preparing to teach 37 percent, making worksheets and activities 33 percent, modifying materials 28 percent. Grading and tutoring penetration is lower and not given a comparable headline percentage. RAND (spring 2025) corroborates the level: 53 percent of English, math, and science teachers used AI for planning and teaching, up from 25 percent the prior year on a changed question. Student side (medium): Gallup and Walton Family Foundation Gen-Z reports about 77 percent of 13-28-year-olds in Heartland states using generative AI, 43 percent weekly (2025), usage not tutoring penetration. No representative US percentage for the share of tutoring delivered by AI exists. Forecast (medium, task-level only): McKinsey (2023): education and training professionals' automated share of hours rises about 16 points with generative AI, from about a 7 percent base to about 23 percent of hours by 2030. No credible 2040 or 2050 education-specific forecast exists; UNESCO and OECD sources discuss policy, not penetration percentage. Plot points: (2024-25, 60 percent, teachers who used AI for work, Gallup and Walton Family Foundation 2025); (2024-25, 32 percent, teachers using AI weekly, Gallup and Walton Family Foundation 2025); (2024-25, 37 percent, teachers using AI monthly or more to prepare to teach, Gallup and Walton Family Foundation 2025); (2030, about 23 percent, education professionals' hours that could be automated, McKinsey 2023, scenario). No credible forecast for 2040 or 2050.

### Forward trajectory

Demographic cliff: the college-age population shrinks in the Northeast and Midwest from about 2026 (Western Interstate Commission for Higher Education, *Knocking at the College Door*); National Center for Education Statistics and actuarial enrollment projections are flat to down. AI tutoring and credential alternatives grow; World Economic Forum and International Monetary Fund exposure for education is lower-displacement and higher-augmentation than finance or law. Trajectory: kindergarten-through-12 skills plateau at a low level with early-grade (age 9) recovery in the 2025 wave; higher-education contraction plus partial trust repair.

Timeline: 1970 tuition 394 dollars, 1978 loan expansion, 1983 *A Nation at Risk*, 2001 No Child Left Behind, 2012 National Assessment of Educational Progress peak, 2023 age-13 reading equal to 1971 level, 2024 debt 1.64 trillion dollars and higher-education confidence at 36 percent low.

## System 6: Media and Information

Symbol versus function: impressions, engagement, and follower counts explode; local reporting capacity, the verifiable-information function, collapses.

### Anchors

- 1987, Federal Communications Commission repeals the Fairness Doctrine; 1996, Telecommunications Act (ownership caps loosened, consolidation wave); 2004-2010, Craigslist, Facebook, and Google absorb classified and local advertising revenue; 2007 onward, the smartphone and social feed become the primary news gateway.
- Local news: since 2005, more than 3,300 newspapers lost by September 2024 (over one third of all), about 3,500 by 2025; print circulation down 65-70 percent; newspaper jobs down 73-75 percent (Medill State of Local News 2024 and 2025, Northwestern University).

### Trust

Gallup mass media: 68-72 percent trust in 1972-1976, 53 percent in 1997, 32 percent low in 2016, 28 percent in 2025, the first sub-30 percent reading and record low; Republicans 8 percent in 2025, Democrats about 51 percent, independents 27 percent. A 2026 Gallup release reported a rebound to 33 percent (Republicans 8 percent rising to 22 percent). Treat as a one-year move, not a trend reversal, until confirmed.

### Current state, 2024-2026

- Medill 2025: 136 newspapers closed in the past year; 213 counties with no local source; more than 1,500 counties with only one; about 50-55 million people with little or no local access. In 2025 Congress cut 1.1 billion dollars for public broadcasting (Corporation for Public Broadcasting), putting local National Public Radio and Public Broadcasting Service stations at risk, per Medill.
- Pew (background, 2024): about 86 percent of Americans get news from a smartphone or computer at least sometimes, and social media is a regular source for about half. Use the Pew *Social Media and News Fact Sheet* primary source for a graphic.

### Automation penetration

Current, measured for web articles (medium, detector-based): Graphite (analysis of more than 55,000-65,000 Common Crawl URLs, three AI detectors, false-positive and false-negative rates under 2 percent claimed): primarily AI-generated articles jumped to 36 percent within 12 months of ChatGPT's launch, 48 percent by 24 months, and have plateaued at about 50 percent since the first quarter of 2025. The crucial qualifier from the same study: AI articles largely do not surface. About 86 percent of articles in Google results are human-written. News-specific (thin): a commercial automated-journalism market report estimates automated content at 8-15 percent of news production by volume at major North American news organizations (financial 25-40 percent, sports 20-35 percent, weather 30-50 percent), forecast to reach 25-35 percent of routine news production by 2033. Single vendor, no methodology published. Plot only as a shaded range with a thin-evidence flag. Newsroom adoption proxy (high, United Kingdom): Reuters Institute (University of Oxford), survey August-November 2024, n equals 1,004 United Kingdom journalists: 56 percent use AI professionally at least weekly, only 16 percent have never used it; monthly use is highest for transcription (49 percent), translation (33 percent), copy-editing (30 percent), and low for generating first drafts (10 percent). No equivalent representative US newsroom survey was found. Contested forecast, do not use as a point: Europol Innovation Lab (2022), "as much as 90 percent of online content may be synthetically generated by 2026." Widely repeated, never measured, and inconsistent with Graphite's 2025-2026 plateau for text. The older Hammond and Narrative Science "90 percent of news by 2030" (2014) is a vendor prediction of the same character. Plot points: (2023, 36 percent, new online articles primarily AI-generated, Graphite); (2024, 48 percent, Graphite); (2025, about 50 percent, new online articles primarily AI-generated, plateau, Graphite); (2025, 8-15 percent, news production automated, major North American news organizations, Dataintelo, thin); (2033, 25-35 percent, routine news production automated, Dataintelo forecast, thin). No credible forecast for 2040 or 2050.

### Forward trajectory

Medill Watch List: 250 counties at high risk (about 40 percent likelihood) of losing their last outlet within a decade (2025). AI-generated content and search AI overviews further cut referral traffic to publishers (Pew 2025 click studies show large drops when AI summaries appear; verify the primary source). Trajectory: accelerating decline for local media, plateau and concentration for national media (a few national brands plus platforms capture the remaining trust and revenue).

Timeline: 1972 media trust about 70 percent, 1987 Fairness Doctrine repealed, 1996 Telecommunications Act, 2005-2025 about 3,500 papers lost, 2025 media trust 28 percent low and Corporation for Public Broadcasting defunded.

## System 7: Housing

Symbol versus function: home values, equity, and "wealth" on paper rise; the function, a median earner buying a median home and forming a household, breaks.

### Anchors

- 1970s: price-to-income about 2.6 times in the late 1960s, about 3.1 times through the 1980s-1990s (Census, National Association of Realtors, and Freddie Mac synthesis). The 30-year mortgage payment share spiked to about 47 percent at the 1981 Volcker peak, then fell for 40 years, masking price divergence.
- 1982-2000s: zoning and permitting constraints tighten in high-productivity metros (academic consensus on direction, magnitude contested); 2000s boom: ratio about 3.9 times, then the 2008 foreclosure crisis (about 10 million foreclosures in standard CoreLogic counts, background).
- 2009-2021, a decade or more of underbuilding after the financial crisis; housing starts never fully catch household formation.
- 2020-2022, pandemic price surge plus rate shock: existing-home sales fell to 1995 lows in 2023 as rates hit about 8 percent.

### Trust and legitimacy

No Gallup housing series exists. Proxy legitimacy measure: Harvard Joint Center for Housing Studies and Census tenure. Homeownership about 65 percent in 1975, peak 69 percent in 2004, about 64-66 percent in 2023-2025. The flat rate despite price explosion means entry age and debt, not the headline rate, carry the decline; the first-time buyer share hit record lows in National Association of Realtors 2024-2025 reports (about 21-24 percent; verify the National Association of Realtors primary source).

### Current state, 2024-2026

- Price-to-income about 4.9-5.0 times in 2024, the highest in the modern series; Harvard Joint Center for Housing Studies: 5 times in 2024, about 5 times in 2025, against about 3.2 times in the 1990s.
- Supply gap. The range is the story: Realtor.com 3.80 million (2024) rising to 4.03 million (2025); Freddie Mac 3.7 million (2024); Zillow 4.7 million; National Association of Home Builders 1.2 million; Dodge vacancy-based 1-1.5 million and narrowing. Flag contested methodology in any graphic. Do not present 4 million as settled.
- Investors: about 25 percent of single-family purchases in the first quarter of 2024 were investors of all sizes, but mega-investors (1,000 or more homes) were about 1 percent of purchases and about 3 percent of single-family rentals and 0.5 percent of all single-family homes nationally (Government Accountability Office and Urban Institute), concentrated locally (for example, about 25 percent of Atlanta single-family rentals in the Government Accountability Office sample). The popular claim that Wall Street bought the housing stock is not supported nationally; local concentration is real.

### Automation penetration

Construction (high, and the surprise of the set): National Association of Home Builders analysis of Census Bureau Survey of Construction: the non-site-built (modular plus panelized and pre-cut) share of single-family completions was 3 percent in 2023, 3 percent in 2024 (28,000 of 1,019,000 homes), and 3 percent in 2025, declining from a 1998 peak of 7 percent. Despite a decade of construction-automation commentary, the measured US curve is flat at 3 percent. Multifamily modular is higher (about 9 percent of starts in 2024 in industry coverage) but from a different, less official series. Three-dimensional-printed homes remain under 1 percent; no Census category exists, so any percentage is anecdotal. Property management and valuation: no official or reputable survey series exists. Aggregator compilations claim 65 percent of property-management companies have implemented AI tenant screening, 48 percent automated lease management, and about 80 percent of tenants pay rent online, all sourced to vendor blogs (DoorLoop, REsimpli, and others), thin and unplottable as fact. Automated valuation (Zillow Zestimate and similar) covers effectively all listed US homes as a data product, but no source quantifies the percentage of valuations or transactions decided by automated valuation models; do not invent one. Forecasts: no credible dated forecast for modular share, construction-robot penetration, or property-management automation to 2030, 2040, or 2050 was found. Vendor market-size compound annual growth rates (modular housing about 8.5 percent revenue growth to 2033) are not penetration forecasts. Plot points: (1998, 7 percent, modular plus panelized share of single-family completions, National Association of Home Builders and Census Survey of Construction, context); (2023, 3 percent); (2024, 3 percent); (2025, 3 percent). No credible forecast for 2030, 2040, or 2050. This is a flat curve until evidence changes.

### Forward trajectory

No forecaster in the set projects price-to-income returning to about 3 times by 2035 without a building boom well above 2025 starts (1.36 million starts in 2025, down 0.6 percent; completions 1.50 million, down 7.9 percent). Trajectory: plateau at an unaffordable level nationally, partial rental easing from 2024-2025 multifamily deliveries (rental vacancy 7.2 percent in the fourth quarter of 2025).

Timeline: about 1970 ratio 2.6 times, 1990s 3.2 times, 2008 crisis, 2010s underbuilding, 2024 ratio 5.0 times with a 3.8 million gap, 2025 gap 4.03 million (Realtor.com method).

## System 8: Labor and Work

Symbol versus function: gross domestic product, productivity, job counts, and "employment" rise; worker share, security, and collective voice fall.

### Anchors

- 1979, productivity and pay decoupling begins. Economic Policy Institute: net productivity up 59.7 percent from 1979 to 2019 against typical worker compensation up 15.8 percent; updated Economic Policy Institute tracker: productivity about 90-94 percent against pay about 33 percent from 1979 to 2025-2026; median wages cumulative up 29 percent since 1979, about one third the productivity pace. If pay had tracked productivity since 1979, typical worker pay would be about 45 percent (about 30,000 dollars) higher (Economic Policy Institute).
- 1981, President Reagan fires striking PATCO air-traffic controllers, a symbolic inflection for private-sector strike and organizing collapse.
- Union density: over 30 percent in the 1950s, 20.1 percent in 1983 (first Bureau of Labor Statistics comparable figure), 10.1 percent in 2022, 10.0 percent in 2025. Private sector 5.9 percent, public sector 32.9-34.3 percent (Bureau of Labor Statistics via Brookings Hamilton Project, *Six Facts*, 2026). Note: the 2025 Current Population Survey is not strictly comparable with prior years; October 2025 was not collected during the federal shutdown, and figures use 11-month averages.
- 2000, labor-force participation peaks above 67 percent; prime-age male participation drifts down (91.5 percent in 2000 falling to about 89 percent recently in cited analysis).
- 2009 onward, gig and app work and the fissured workplace (David Weil), with schedule and benefits detached from the "employee" symbol.

### Trust and legitimacy

Gallup organized-labor confidence 26 percent (2024-2025 releases), low as confidence, but Gallup approval of unions (a separate question) has been high (about 68-70 percent in 2022-2025 reporting). Do not conflate the two series. Legitimacy paradox: the public approves of unions, while almost no private workers are in one.

### Current state, 2024-2026

- Union members 14.7 million in 2025, up about 400,000 against 2024, yet density flat at 10.0 percent because employment grew; National Labor Relations Board election petitions fell about 350 cases in fiscal year 2025 after a fiscal year 2024 decade high (Hamilton and Brookings).
- Overall labor-force participation rate about 61.5 percent in June 2026 reporting, the lowest outside the pandemic since 1976, about two thirds explained by aging; prime-age participation about 83-84 percent. Youth (ages 16-24) participation collapsed from about 66 percent (2000) to the mid-50s.

### Automation penetration

This is the best-evidenced system and the source of the proxy curves for the others. Current and near forecast (high): World Economic Forum *Future of Jobs Report 2025* (survey of more than 1,000 employers, 14 million workers): today 47 percent of work tasks are performed mainly by humans, 22 percent mainly by technology, 30 percent by a combination; employers expect by 2030: 33 percent human-only, 34 percent technology-only, 33 percent combination. Also: 170 million jobs created and 92 million displaced by 2030 (net plus 78 million, 22 percent of jobs churned). Adoption scenario (high): McKinsey Global Institute (July 2023), *Generative AI and the Future of Work in America*: by 2030, activities accounting for up to 30 percent of US hours worked could be automated, 29.5 percent with generative AI against 21.5 percent without. Long horizon (high, scenario): McKinsey (2023 update of *A Future That Works*): with generative AI, 50 percent of today's work activities could be automated between 2030 and 2060, midpoint 2045 (about a decade earlier than the pre-generative-AI estimate). Technical potential today (high, not a forecast): McKinsey (November 2025), *Agents, Robots, and Us*: demonstrated technology could in theory automate 57 percent of US work hours (44 percent by agents, 13 percent by robots); roles with the highest potential are about 40 percent of jobs. Exposure ceilings (high): Goldman Sachs (March 2023): about two thirds of US and European occupations exposed to some AI automation, about 25 percent of all work could be done by generative AI, 7 percent of US workers in jobs where at least half of tasks could be done by generative AI. International Monetary Fund (January 2024): about 40 percent of global employment exposed, about 60 percent in advanced economies, roughly half of exposed jobs likely to benefit from complementarity. Physical automation (high): International Federation of Robotics, *World Robotics 2025*: US robot density 295 per 10,000 manufacturing employees (2023) rising to 307 (2024), eighth globally (Korea 1,220; global average 132-162 depending on edition basis). International Federation of Robotics forecasts global installations up 6 percent to 575,000 in 2025, passing 700,000 by 2028 (units, not percentage). Warehouse case (medium): Amazon, 750,000 robots (2023-2024) rising to the one-millionth robot deployed in June 2025 across more than 300 sites; Amazon says about 75 percent of deliveries are robot-assisted in some part. Leaked internal plans reported in October 2025 to automate up to 75 percent of operations and avoid more than 600,000 hires by 2033 are reported, not confirmed; Amazon disputes the framing, so flag if used. Plot points, share of work tasks and hours automated (technology-only and automated hours): (2025, 22 percent, tasks performed mainly by technology, World Economic Forum Future of Jobs 2025); (2030, 34 percent, tasks performed mainly by technology, World Economic Forum 2025 forecast); (2030, 29.5 percent, US hours that could be automated with generative AI, McKinsey 2023 scenario); (2025, 57 percent, US hours technically automatable, McKinsey 2025, ceiling, do not plot on the same line as adoption); (2045, 50 percent, work activities automated, midpoint scenario with range 2030-2060, McKinsey 2023); (2050, no point forecast; the McKinsey range implies 50 percent is reached somewhere in 2030-2060).

### Forward trajectory

World Economic Forum 2025 employer survey: 41 percent of employers intend workforce reductions from AI automation by 2030, even in a net-positive jobs scenario. Bureau of Labor Statistics Employment Projections (2023-2033 and 2024-2034) show healthcare and social assistance dominating growth, and office and administrative support shrinking. Trajectory: plateau for density and participation, accelerating task restructuring.

Timeline: 1979 decoupling, 1981 PATCO, 1983 union density 20.1 percent, 2000 labor-force participation peak 67 percent, 2025 union density 10.0 percent and private-sector density 5.9 percent.

## System 9: Food System

Symbol versus function: calories, choice, "food" stock-keeping units, and convenience at historic abundance; nutritional quality, farm viability, and food security deteriorate.

### Anchors

- 1971, Earl Butz "get big or get out" and the 1973 Agriculture Act, the cheap-calorie consolidation era begins; 1977 and 1980, the first Dietary Guidelines, as the obesity inflection begins.
- Obesity (CDC National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey, background series; verify the National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey table for a graphic): adult obesity about 15 percent in 1976-1980, about 23 percent in 1988-1994, about 42 percent in 2017-2020, 40.3 percent in 2021-2023. The 2021-2023 dip and stabilization is the first non-rise in the series and coincides with GLP-1 uptake, contested as trend versus noise.
- Consolidation: the four largest beef packers' share of steer and heifer purchases, 36 percent in 1980, 81 percent in 1995, about 85 percent (2019 US Department of Agriculture Economic Research Service data), Tyson, JBS, Cargill, and National Beef. Pork top four: 34 percent rising to 67 percent (US Department of Agriculture Economic Research Service via Investigate Midwest fact-check).
- Ultra-processed food becomes the majority diet. Measurement starts in the 2000s (NOVA classification); by 2017-2018 ultra-processed food was about 57 percent of adult calories in Journal of the American Medical Association and National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey analyses (background).

### Trust

No long institutional trust series for the food system exists. Edelman 2025 sector data put food and beverage among more-trusted industries (about 73 percent for the healthcare sector in the United States in the cited summary; food similar). Trust in brands and regulators (Food and Drug Administration and US Department of Agriculture) is lower and partisan-volatile after 2024. Flag as thin.

### Current state, 2024-2026

- CDC National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey, August 2021-August 2023 (released 2025): ultra-processed food equals 55 percent of calories for ages 1 and up, 53 percent for adults, 61.9 percent for youth.
- Food insecurity (US Department of Agriculture Economic Research Service): 10.2 percent of households in 2021 (historic low), 12.8 percent in 2022, 13.5 percent in 2023, 13.7 percent in 2024 (18.3 million households, about 48 million people; 5.4 percent very low food security). The US Department of Agriculture announced in September 2025 that it would discontinue the annual report after 2024. The measurement symbol itself is being retired, noted by the Alliance to End Hunger.
- Adult obesity 40.3 percent, youth 21.1 percent in 2021-2023 (National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey); no state had a statistically significant increase from 2024 to 2025 in the Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System-based Trust for America's Health 2026 report, and two decreased, an early plateau signal.

### Automation penetration

Farm automation (high via US Department of Agriculture, reported secondarily): US Department of Agriculture Economic Research Service and National Agricultural Statistics Service: 27 percent of US farms used precision-agriculture practices in 2023 (a 2025 National Agricultural Statistics Service cut put the figure at 22 percent on a changed basis; note the discrepancy and do not splice). By technology and farm size the picture is far ahead: GPS autosteer guidance on 70 percent of large-scale crop farms and 52 percent of midsize farms (2023), up from 34 percent guidance adoption on soybean farms in 2012. Robotic milking: about 6 percent of US milk production (2021, US Department of Agriculture Economic Research Service). Food processing: no reputable penetration percentage exists. Evidence is market-size only (for example, food-processing automation robotics revenue, meat-processing robots about 0.45 billion dollars in 2024 growing about 12 percent compound annual growth rate to 2032 in vendor estimates) and qualitative (labor shortages, vision-guided deboning pilots at Tyson, Perdue, and Pilgrim's). Variable, irregular products are the stated technical brake. Say so; do not convert revenue to a percentage. Automated retail and checkout (thin and contested): a market report estimates about 30 percent of retail transactions used self-checkout in 2024. Counter-evidence: Dollar General removed or converted self-checkout at about 12,000 stores in 2024 over shrink, and retailer investment intent fell in 2026 surveys. This sub-curve may be declining, an important honest exception to monotonic adoption. Plot points: (2023, 27 percent, US farms using precision-agriculture practices, US Department of Agriculture Economic Research Service and National Agricultural Statistics Service); (2023, 70 percent, large crop farms using GPS autosteer, US Department of Agriculture Economic Research Service); (2023, 52 percent, midsize crop farms using GPS autosteer, US Department of Agriculture Economic Research Service); (2024, about 30 percent, retail transactions via self-checkout, WiseGuyReports market estimate, thin, contested by retailer retreat). No credible penetration forecast for farm, processing, or checkout automation in 2030, 2040, or 2050.

### Forward trajectory

GLP-1 drugs are the largest credible discontinuity: analysts (Morgan Stanley and others, secondary) project US users could reach about 10 percent of the population by the mid-2030s with measurable cuts to calorie demand. Magnitudes vary widely; flag as contested. US Department of Agriculture and Economic Research Service long-run projections assume continued consolidation and flat farm numbers. Trajectory: possible partial repair on obesity via pharmacology (pay to restore function), continued decline on security and consolidation absent policy change.

Timeline: 1971-1973 get-big era, 1980 beef top four 36 percent, about 2000 ultra-processed food becomes the majority diet, 2021 food insecurity low 10.2 percent, 2024 food insecurity 13.7 percent with ultra-processed food at 55 percent of calories, 2025 US Department of Agriculture hunger report discontinued.

## System 10: Community and Social Fabric

Symbol versus function: "friends," followers, and group memberships online; in-person time, confidants, civic organizations, and shared institutions thin out. This system has the cleanest time-use evidence.

### Anchors

- 1972, generalized trust: 45 percent of Americans said most people can be trusted (General Social Survey, cited in the Surgeon General advisory), falling to about 30 percent in 2016.
- 1960s-2000, Putnam, *Bowling Alone* (2000): civic, religious, union, Parent-Teacher Association, and bowling-league membership and informal socializing fell from mid-1960s peaks across dozens of series; television time was Putnam's leading correlate. Critics noted new forms (support groups, large advocacy non-governmental organizations); Putnam's reply, and later data, show those did not offset the in-person decline.
- Church membership (Gallup): about 70 percent from 1937 to 1999, 50 percent in 2018, 47 percent in 2020, the first reading below 50 percent in Gallup history; religious "nones" 8 percent in 1998-2000 rising to 21 percent in 2018-2020.
- 2007 onward, smartphone and social-media adolescence begins (iPhone 2007, Instagram 2010). Timing matches youth distress and friend-time acceleration; causation is actively debated (Jonathan Haidt against Candice Odgers and co-authors in *Nature* 2024; flag as contested).

### Trust

Interpersonal trust above; institutional trust in the cross-system summary. Only 16 percent felt "very attached" to their community in 2018 (cited in the Surgeon General advisory).

### Current state, 2024-2026

- Surgeon General Advisory (2023, the latest federal synthesis): time with friends in person, 60 minutes per day (30 hours per month) in 2003 falling to 20 minutes per day (10 hours per month) in 2020; time alone up 24 hours per month; ages 15-24 friend time 150 minutes falling to 40 minutes per day (down about 70 percent); 49 percent reported three or fewer close friends in 2021 against 27 percent in 1990; solo-person households 13 percent in 1960 rising to 29 percent in 2022. Social disconnection mortality risk compared by the Surgeon General to smoking up to 15 cigarettes per day (meta-analytic Holt-Lunstad figure).
- Fertility and marriage (context, Census and CDC background): fertility about 1.6 in 2023-2024, a record low. Include only with the CDC National Center for Health Statistics primary source in a final graphic.

### Automation penetration

Current (high, teens only): Common Sense Media, *Talk, Trust, and Trade-Offs* (survey April 30-May 14, 2025, by NORC, n equals 1,060 US teens ages 13-17): 72 percent have used an AI companion at least once; 52 percent are regular users (a few times a month or more); 13 percent daily. Thirty-three percent of teen users use them for social interaction and relationships (18 percent conversation and social practice, 12 percent emotional and mental-health support, 9 percent as a friend, 8 percent romantic or flirtatious). About one in three teen users had discussed something serious with an AI companion instead of a person; about 20 percent spend as much or more time with AI companions as with real friends (per study summaries). Adults (thin): a 2025 Brigham Young University study reported via secondary coverage, about one in five US adults has used a chatbot to simulate a romantic partner (31 percent of young men, 23 percent of young women). No primary report was retrieved; flag as thin. Platform scale (context, not percentage): Character.AI about 20 million monthly active users reported in late 2025; Replika over 10-25 million users in vendor and secondary claims, inconsistent, do not plot. Forecasts: no credible forecast exists for the share of social interaction mediated by AI companions in 2030, 2040, or 2050. AI-companion market revenue forecasts (for example, 37.7 billion dollars in 2025, about 31 percent compound annual growth rate to 2034 in secondary coverage) are vendor figures, not interaction shares. Plot points for US teens ages 13-17: (2025, 72 percent, ever used an AI companion, Common Sense Media and NORC); (2025, 52 percent, regular AI-companion users, Common Sense Media and NORC); (2025, 33 percent, teen users using companions for social interaction and relationships, Common Sense Media and NORC). No credible forecast for 2030, 2040, or 2050; the adult population baseline is not established.

### Forward trajectory

No official social-capital forecast exists. Forward indicators are cohort-based: each younger cohort reports fewer confidants, lower organizational membership, and more solo living at the same age, implying continued decline as cohorts replace one another unless in-person infrastructure or policy intervenes. AI companions (2023 onward) are a new, unmeasured substitution risk. Evidence is thin; do not quantify.

Timeline: 1972 trust 45 percent, 2000 *Bowling Alone*, 2003-2020 friend time down two thirds, 2020 church membership under 50 percent, 2023 Surgeon General loneliness epidemic advisory.

## System 11: Governance and Democracy

Symbol versus function: elections, filings, spending, and "participation" (small-dollar counts, turnout in polarized peaks) stay high; trust, shared legitimacy of outcomes, and governing capacity fall.

### Anchors

- 1971, Federal Election Campaign Act; 1974 post-Watergate reforms; 1976, Buckley v. Valeo (money-as-speech framework); 1978, Proposition 13 era and tax revolt (state-level capacity inflection).
- 1990s-2000s: congressional approval and Gallup confidence collapse in steps with shutdowns (1995-1996), impeachment, Iraq, and the financial crisis.
- 2010, Citizens United and SpeechNow (super political action committee era); 2013, Shelby County v. Holder (Voting Rights Act preclearance struck down); 2020-2021, election denial and January 6: losing-side confidence in counts collapses, then partially recovers when that side wins (see current state; legitimacy is now conditional and partisan, the key structural change).
- Debt and governance link: see System 2. The Congressional Budget Office baseline assumes current law and still reaches 156 percent of GDP by 2055.

### Trust, the longest series in the report

Pew trust in the federal government "always or most of the time": 73 percent in 1958, about 35 percent in the post-Watergate era (background midpoint), 22 percent in 2024, 17 percent in 2025 (2 percent always plus 15 percent most), near the lowest in about 70 years; Democrats 35 percent falling to 9 percent in 2025 (their record low), Republicans 26 percent (Pew via FEDweek). Gallup Congress confidence: 42 percent in 1973 (in early Gallup summaries), 7 percent in 2022, 9-11 percent in 2024-2025, the lowest of any institution every recent year. Gallup out-party trust: opponents trusting the federal government on domestic problems, 54 percent in the 1970s falling to 18 percent today; executive branch 49 percent falling to 7 percent.

### Current state, 2024-2026

- Turnout paradox: 2020 turnout 66 percent (highest since 1908), 2024 turnout 64 percent (tied second-highest, Pew). Participation is strong while trust is at lows; Pew attributes the spike partly to polarization raising perceived stakes.
- Election confidence is winner-contingent: Republican confidence in in-person counts 64 percent (2020) rising to 94 percent (2024); mail-ballot confidence 20 percent rising to 72 percent; Democratic confidence fell in 2024 but stayed in the majority. Pew, October 2026: in-person count confidence 79 percent overall, mail-ballot 53 percent, with 45-point partisan gaps on noncitizen-voting prevention.

### Automation penetration

Current activity (high): Government Accountability Office, *Generative AI Use and Management at Federal Agencies* (GAO-25-107653, July 2025): across 11 agencies with inventories, reported AI use cases nearly doubled, 571 (2023) rising to 1,110 (2024); generative-AI use cases rose about ninefold, 32 rising to 282. Sixty-one percent of use cases are mission-enabling or internal support, not public-facing services. The Department of Health and Human Services (271), Department of Veterans Affairs (229), and Department of Homeland Security (183) lead. The consolidated Office of Management and Budget inventory listed 1,757 public AI uses across 37 agencies for 2024, a count, again not a share. Digital-service baseline (high, old): Information Technology and Innovation Foundation (August 2021) audit of 1,348 federal forms: only 2 percent fully compliant as web-based forms under the 21st Century Integrated Digital Experience Act; 78 percent partially compliant (fillable PDF). The Federal Chief Information Officer (2023) repeated the 2 percent figure and 38 billion dollars per year in paper spending. No updated percentage audit was found for 2024-2025; the 2021 point is the last measured baseline, which is itself a finding. Automated decisions in benefits and permits: no US government statistic quantifies the percentage of benefit, permit, or eligibility decisions made or materially decided by automated systems. This is the largest evidence gap in the set. Academic and advocacy inventories document individual systems (for example, state unemployment and benefits eligibility) without a denominator. Forecasts: no credible dated forecast for the percentage of government services or decisions automated by 2030, 2040, or 2050 exists. Agency targets found are process targets (for example, General Services Administration cloud and Federal Risk and Authorization Management Program timelines), not automation-penetration forecasts. Plot points: (2021, 2 percent, federal forms fully digital and IDEA-compliant, Information Technology and Innovation Foundation); (2021, 78 percent, federal forms partially digital, fillable PDF, Information Technology and Innovation Foundation); (2023, 571, reported federal AI use cases, 11 agencies, Government Accountability Office, count, plot on a secondary axis only); (2024, 1,110, reported federal AI use cases, 11 agencies, Government Accountability Office, count); (2024, 282, of which generative AI, Government Accountability Office, count). No credible percentage forecast for 2030, 2040, or 2050.

### Forward trajectory

Freedom House, Economist Intelligence Unit, and Varieties of Democracy scores for the United States have trended down since about 2010 (Freedom House: United States about 83-84 of 100 in recent years against 94 in the mid-2000s; verify the Freedom House primary source for a graphic; direction is cross-source consistent, exact points differ by index). Congressional Budget Office demographic anchor: without immigration, the US population begins shrinking in 2033 (Congressional Budget Office 2025). Trajectory: plateau at low legitimacy with high mobilization, "high-turnout, low-trust democracy." Not a forecast of democratic breakdown, which no source in this set predicts as the base case.

Timeline: 1958 trust 73 percent, 1976 Buckley v. Valeo, 2010 Citizens United, 2013 Shelby County, 2021 January 6, 2024 turnout 64 percent with trust at 22 percent, 2025 trust 17 percent.

## System 12: Energy, Emotional Energy and Attention (Burnout, Exhaustion)

Note on definition: this is not the power sector. It is human attentional and psychic energy. There is no 1970s baseline survey for "burnout" (term coined 1974, Herbert Freudenberger; Maslach Burnout Inventory 1981). Any 1970 start point for this graph must be labeled as a proxy (work hours, sleep, television time), not burnout itself. Flag prominently.

### Anchors and proxies

- 1970s-1990s: average annual work hours per employed person stop falling and plateau or rise for dual-earner households (background; Bureau of Labor Statistics and Juliet Schor, *The Overworked American*, contested by Robinson time-diary data showing leisure rose; flag as classic contested literature).
- 2004, attention baseline (Gloria Mark, University of California Irvine): average attention on a screen about 2.5 minutes, falling to 75 seconds by about 2012 and 47 seconds in recent years in Mark's logging studies. Widely repeated; the sample is knowledge workers, not the population. Do not present as a national average.
- 2007-2016, always-on era: smartphone plus push notification plus algorithmic feed. US employees' reported stress rises in the Gallup and American Psychological Association Stress in America series (American Psychological Association series starts 2007).
- 2020-2021, pandemic burnout peak: World Health Organization ICD-11 burnout as an occupational phenomenon (2019, effective 2022) legitimates the symbol just as healthcare and essential-worker exhaustion spikes.

### Trust and legitimacy proxy

Gallup engagement is the closest longitudinal "energy at work" series: US employee engagement peaked at 36 percent in 2020 in the Gallup trend (background) and fell thereafter.

### Current state, 2024-2026

- Gallup *State of the Global Workplace*: global engagement 23 percent falling to 21 percent in 2024 (report 2025), cost 438 billion dollars; 2026 report: global engagement 20 percent in 2025, manager engagement 27 percent falling to 22 percent in one year (31 percent in 2022 falling to 22 percent in 2025), low-engagement cost about 10 trillion dollars (about 9 percent of global GDP). US engagement 31 percent, the lowest in a decade; 17 percent actively disengaged (Gallup via summaries).
- Attention and screen: US average screen time about 7 hours 3 minutes per day in 2024 (DataReportal-based secondary); phone checks 205 per day in the 2024 Reviews.org survey (up from 144 in 2023), a self-report and screen-report survey of 1,000 adults, not passive logging, flag the method. Teens averaged 4.8 hours per day on social media in 2023 (Gallup, Families and Tech, and Surgeon General-cited data, background).

### Automation penetration

Two unrelated systems share this slot. Keep them as two separate curves.

Grid and metering automation (high): Federal Energy Regulatory Commission, *2024 Assessment of Demand Response and Advanced Metering* (Energy Information Administration data): 119.3 million advanced meters in operation in 2022, of 165 million total meters, equal to 72.3 percent penetration; residential penetration passed 70 percent for the first time; the Pacific, South Atlantic, and West South Central divisions above 80 percent. ### Forward trajectory

No forecaster projects engagement or attention recovery as the base case. Gallup's own framing: manager squeeze (span of control 10.9 rising to 12.1 direct reports from 2024 to 2025, about 50 percent higher since 2013) plus AI and return-to-office load predicts further manager disengagement feeding team turnover in the next 12 months. AI "productivity" tools cut task time but add monitoring and notification load in early studies; the net energy effect is contested and thin. Trajectory: accelerating depletion at the manager layer, plateau-high exhaustion overall.

Timeline: 1974 "burnout" coined, 1981 Maslach Burnout Inventory, 2004 screen attention 2.5 minutes, 2020 US engagement peak 36 percent, 2024 global engagement 21 percent, 2025 manager engagement 22 percent and global engagement 20 percent.

## System 13: Entertainment

Symbol versus function: franchise grosses, streaming hours, and catalog size at records; per-capita theatrical attendance, original films, and per-unit artist income collapse.

### Anchors

- 1975, *Jaws*, and 1977, *Star Wars*: the blockbuster and franchise model is born; 1980s, home video first unbundles the theatre.
- Theatrical attendance per capita (North America, The Numbers and National Association of Theatre Owners background series): about 4 or more tickets per person per year in the early 1970s era of the series' modern form and in the 2002 peak (1.6 billion tickets, about 5.5 per capita including Canada in some series; series definitions vary, verify). Falling to 1.24 billion tickets in 2019 (about 3.3 US per capita in the cited summary). Use admissions, not gross, for any graphic; gross is inflated by price.
- 1999-2000, Napster and file-sharing; recorded-music revenue (Recording Industry Association of America): US revenue peaked in 1999 (about 14.6 billion dollars nominal) and fell about 50 percent to a trough around 2014, before streaming rebuilt nominal revenue with a different distribution (background Recording Industry Association of America series).
- 2007, Netflix streaming; 2019, Disney Plus and the streaming-wars peak-content era; 2008, Marvel Cinematic Universe begins, franchise-universe dominance.

### Trust and legitimacy

No Gallup entertainment series exists. Proxy: willingness to pay and attend (below), and Gallup large-tech confidence, 32 percent in 2020 falling to 20 percent record low in the 2025 release. Platforms that now are entertainment distribution are distrusted even as use rises.

### Current state, 2024-2026

- Admissions: about 883 million in 2024, about 769 million in 2025 (cited Pew and Comscore-based summary); domestic box office about 8.6-8.9 billion dollars in 2025, still about 20-23 percent below 2019 despite higher prices; ticket sales down 46 percent against the early-2000s peak, down about 40 percent against 2019. Only 53 percent of US adults visited a theatre at all in the past year (Pew, cited 2025-2026).
- Originality: original films were about 19 percent of the Top-25 domestic box office in the early 2010s, under 3 percent in 2019, and low single digits or about zero in some post-COVID years (studio-research-based Substack series, single-source, flag). Franchise share above 50 percent of major-studio wide releases by 2025 in the cited slate analysis.
- Music: Spotify paid over 10 billion dollars to the industry in 2024 in aggregate, but per-stream 0.003-0.005 dollars; independent-artist average 4.04 dollars per 1,000 streams in 2021 falling to 3.41 dollars in 2024 (Duetti), or 340 dollars per 100,000 streams before splits. Concert grosses and prices simultaneously at records (Pollstar background). Live becomes a luxury good; recorded music becomes a near-free symbol.

### Automation penetration

The metric that fits best in all of media: the percentage of new music uploads that are fully AI-generated (Deezer, measured) against the percentage of streams (consumption). Current, measured (high, the platform's own detection data): Deezer (the only major streamer that tags fully AI-generated content): fully AI-generated tracks were about 10 percent of daily deliveries in January 2025 (about 10,000 per day), 18 percent in April 2025, 28 percent in September 2025 (over 30,000 per day), 34 percent by end-2025, 44 percent in April 2026 (about 75,000 per day; 13.4 million AI tracks tagged in 2025). Consumption gap (high): AI music was only 0.5 percent of Deezer streams in September 2025, rising to a reported 1-3 percent of streams in 2026 coverage, with about 70 percent (September 2025 basis) to 85 percent (2026 basis) of AI-music streams detected as fraudulent and demonetized. Any entertainment curve that plots uploads without streams overstates lived penetration by an order of magnitude. Other entertainment: no comparable measured series exists for film, television, or games. Spotify removed 75 million spam and AI tracks in its September 2025 crackdown (count, no percentage). Deezer and Ipsos (2025): 97 percent of listeners could not distinguish AI from human music in blind tests, a capability datapoint, not penetration. Forecasts: no credible analyst forecast for the AI share of music streams, viewing hours, or entertainment output in 2030, 2040, or 2050 was found. Prediction-market prices (for example, Manifold: 64 percent chance most digital entertainment is AI-generated by 2040) are not analyst forecasts and should only be used, if at all, labeled as crowd odds. Plot points, fully AI-generated share of Deezer daily music uploads: (January 2025, 10 percent); (April 2025, 18 percent); (September 2025, 28 percent); (December 2025, 34 percent); (April 2026, 44 percent); (September 2025, 0.5 percent, AI share of Deezer streams, Deezer, plot as a second line). No credible forecast for 2030, 2040, or 2050.

### Forward trajectory

No credible forecast restores per-capita theatrical attendance to 2019, let alone 2002, levels; exhibitor and analyst consensus in the trade press is a permanently smaller theatrical market (about 9 billion dollars domestic ceiling in near-term forecasts) concentrated in premium screens and event films. Streaming consolidation plus the AI music and video supply flood further compress median creator income even if total industry revenue grows (International Federation of the Phonographic Industry and Recording Industry Association of America outlooks are revenue-positive and distribution-negative). Trajectory: plateau at low attendance, accelerating concentration (top franchises and the top 1 percent of artists capture a rising share).

Timeline: 1975 *Jaws*, 1999 recorded revenue peak and Napster, 2002 admissions peak 1.6 billion, 2007 streaming, 2019 originals under 3 percent of the Top 25, 2025 admissions about 769 million and box office down 23 percent against 2019.

# Cross-System Inflection Clusters

The trajectories do not move independently. Four clusters recur across the systems.

## Cluster 1: 1971-1983, deregulation, financialization, and the measurement shift

Gallup average institutional confidence starts at 40-50 percent in the 1980s-1990s, at the low end of a postwar peak. The same years carry: the 1971 Nixon Shock and the Federal Election Campaign Act; the 1973 Agriculture Act and the cheap-calorie turn; the 1978 loan expansion; the 1981 PATCO firing and the start of the productivity and pay decoupling (1979); the 1982-1983 union-density and *A Nation at Risk* moments; and the 1980s peak of bank counts and of the public-school confidence series. The 1970s are the last decade in which most systems are measured near their long-run trust and function peaks. The inflection is not a single event but a cluster of policy moves that rewrote who extracts value and who bears cost, in finance, food, labor, education, and governance at once.

## Cluster 2: 1996-2010, consolidation and platform capture

The 1996 Telecommunications Act and the 1996 Personal Responsibility and Work Opportunity Reconciliation Act (welfare reform) sit alongside the 1999 financial deregulation (Gramm-Leach-Bliley) and the 1999 recorded-music revenue peak, the 2000 labor-force participation peak, the 2004-2010 collapse of classified and local advertising into platforms, the 2006-2007 mortgage-securitization peak, and the 2008 financial crisis and foreclosure wave. In this window, consolidation reaches its modern form in food (beef top four 85 percent), media (newspaper losses accelerate), and finance (bank count collapses), while the platform layer, not the institution it displaced, begins to capture the revenue and the attention. The 2008 crisis is the clearest case where a system's symbol (AAA-rated mortgage securities) and function (creditworthiness) diverged catastrophically at the same moment.

## Cluster 3: 2020 accelerant, pandemic and generative AI

COVID-19 compresses a decade of movement into two years. Friend time falls to 20 minutes per day (2020), trust falls to record lows across Gallup and Pew series, theatrical admissions collapse and never recover to 2019, food insecurity jumps from 10.2 percent to 12.8-13.7 percent, and the remote-work and platform shift becomes permanent. Then ChatGPT (November 2022) opens the generative-AI acceleration: Deezer AI uploads from 10 percent (January 2025) to 44 percent (April 2026), AI articles plateau at about 50 percent of new web articles, teacher AI use reaches 60 percent, physician AI use reaches 66 percent, and federal AI use cases double in one year. The 2020-2026 window is where the automation curves in this report actually move, while the trust and function curves mostly plateau at their lows rather than recovering.

## Cluster 4: The trust cascade, 2015-2026

Gallup average institutional confidence falls from the 40-50 percent band to 28 percent in 2025 and 27 percent in 2026, the lowest readings on record. Every institution falls in the same direction over the decade, with the steepest drops in the presidency (43 percent to 27 percent), the Supreme Court (46 percent to 27 percent), newspapers and television news, and Congress. The cascade is partisan in form: the same institution gains trust when its side holds it and loses it when the other side does. Pew trust in the federal government reaches 17 percent in 2025, near the lowest in about 70 years. The cascade is the cross-system summary statistic of the whole report: the symbol systems keep operating while belief in them falls to levels at which their own legitimacy becomes the constraint.

# Automation Outlook

## What is measured and what is not

The automation evidence in this report is uneven by design. Labor has real measured curves (World Economic Forum task shares, McKinsey adoption scenarios). Entertainment has one measured supply curve (Deezer AI uploads) and a measured consumption gap (streams at 0.5-3 percent). Media has one measured curve (Graphite AI articles) with a measured visibility gap (86 percent of Google results are human-written). Energy has two measured curves (advanced meters at 72.3 percent, unconnected AI-recommended feed share at 20-40 percent). Healthcare, law, and education have adoption snapshots (physician AI use 66 percent, teacher AI use 60 percent, prior-authorization automation 21 percent), not penetration curves. Finance, housing, food, community, and governance have no credible penetration percentage at all, only market-size estimates, vendor claims, or isolated counts. The honest summary: automation is measured where platforms count it, and unmeasured where institutions would have to admit it.

## Forecaster disagreement

Three forecasters disagree in a structured way, and the disagreement is the finding.

| Forecaster | Method | Horizon | Headline | Character |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| World Economic Forum, Future of Jobs 2025 | Employer survey (more than 1,000 employers, 14 million workers) | 2030 | Tasks mainly by technology: 22 percent (2025) rising to 34 percent (2030); 170 million jobs created, 92 million displaced, net plus 78 million | Adoption intent, optimistic on net jobs |
| McKinsey Global Institute, 2023 | Activity-level technical analysis plus adoption modeling | 2030-2060, midpoint 2045 | Up to 30 percent of US hours automated by 2030 (with generative AI); 50 percent of work activities automated between 2030 and 2060 | Scenario range, midpoint anchor |
| McKinsey, Agents, Robots, and Us, November 2025 | Demonstrated-technology assessment | Today | 57 percent of US work hours technically automatable (44 percent agents, 13 percent robots) | Technical ceiling, not a forecast |
| Goldman Sachs, March 2023 | Occupational exposure analysis | No date | About two thirds of occupations exposed; about 25 percent of all work could be done by generative AI; 7 percent of US workers in jobs where at least half of tasks could be done | Exposure ceiling |
| International Monetary Fund, January 2024 | Occupational exposure analysis | No date | About 40 percent of global employment exposed, about 60 percent in advanced economies; roughly half of exposed jobs likely to benefit | Exposure with complementarity split |
| Oxford Martin School and Citi, 2016 | Task-level automatability (Frey and Osborne method) | 2035 | 47 percent of US jobs at risk (older estimate); 57 percent of OECD jobs at risk in the Citi variant | High-risk framing, older method, contested |

The pattern: exposure and technical-potential estimates (57 percent, two thirds, 47 percent) are ceilings. Employer adoption surveys (World Economic Forum) and activity-level adoption models (McKinsey) give lower, dated numbers. No forecaster in this set gives a credible per-system penetration forecast for 2040 or 2050. The McKinsey 2045 midpoint is the only defensible long-horizon anchor, and it applies to labor only.

## The McKinsey 2045 midpoint anchor

McKinsey (2023 update of *A Future That Works*): with generative AI, 50 percent of today's work activities could be automated between 2030 and 2060, midpoint 2045, about a decade earlier than the pre-generative-AI estimate. This is a scenario range with a midpoint, not a point forecast. It is the longest credible horizon in the set, and it is labor-specific. Do not apply it to other systems.

## Measurement warnings

- Task exposure is not job loss. Goldman Sachs and the International Monetary Fund measure exposure; the World Economic Forum measures employer intent; McKinsey measures technical potential and modeled adoption. These are different quantities and must not be plotted on one line.
- Supply is not consumption. Deezer AI uploads at 44 percent against AI streams at 0.5-3 percent, and Graphite AI articles at about 50 percent against 86 percent human-written Google results, show the same gap: automated supply floods the catalog while human output still dominates what people actually see and hear.
- Counts are not shares. Federal AI use cases (1,110 in 2024) and robot counts (Amazon one millionth robot, June 2025) are numerators without denominators. Do not convert them into percentages.
- Vendor market-size compound annual growth rates are not penetration forecasts. Modular-housing revenue growth, food-automation robotics revenue, and AI-companion market size tell nothing about the share of homes, meals, or conversations that are automated.
- The 2020s acceleration is real but narrow. The steepest measured automation curves in this report (Deezer uploads, AI articles, physician AI use, teacher AI use, federal AI use cases) all move in 2023-2026. The flat curves (modular housing at 3 percent, housing supply gap, union density) do not move at all. Automation is accelerating where the product is digital and countable, and flat where the product is physical, institutional, or collective.

# Open Questions and What Could Not Be Verified

1. **Per-system 2040 and 2050 forecasts.** No credible forecast exists for any of the thirteen systems at 2040 or 2050, except the McKinsey labor midpoint (2045). Any timeline that draws a line to 2050 for a system other than labor is extrapolating, and must say so.
2. **The integrity index.** The "integrity index" does not appear in either source report. It is an artifact-layer construct, a synthesized comparative shape with no basis in the cited research. If it appears in a chart or timeline, it must be labeled as synthesized, not as a research finding.
3. **Government automation share.** No US government statistic quantifies the percentage of benefit, permit, or eligibility decisions made or materially decided by automated systems. This is the largest evidence gap in the set.
4. **Finance automation share.** No credible source quantifies the percentage of financial decisions, credit decisions, or transactions that are automated. Market-size and adoption-claim figures are vendor estimates.
5. **Food-processing automation share.** No reputable penetration percentage exists for food processing. Revenue estimates are not penetration.
6. **Property-management automation share.** No official or reputable survey series exists. Vendor-blog percentages (65 percent tenant screening, 48 percent lease management) are thin and unplottable as fact.
7. **Adult AI-companion use.** No primary US adult baseline was retrieved. The Brigham Young University figure (about one in five adults) is secondary coverage only. Teen use is well measured (Common Sense Media and NORC, 2025); adult use is not.
8. **Union-density 2025 comparability.** The 2025 Current Population Survey is not strictly comparable with prior years; October 2025 was not collected during the federal shutdown, and figures use 11-month averages. Treat the 10.0 percent reading as approximate.
9. **Bank count 1984.** The Federal Deposit Insurance Corporation and CEIC series peaks at 14,496 institutions; the source report's 14,483 reflects a 13-bank vintage and definition difference. Either figure is defensible with its source named; do not present the difference as a trend.
10. **Gallup average-confidence endpoint years.** The 2025 average is 28 percent (July 17, 2025 release). The 27 percent average is the July 2026 release (survey June 1-15, 2026). The 2023 record low was 26 percent. Any chart or table that labels 27 percent as 2025 is misdated.
11. **The 2026 Gallup media-trust rebound.** Gallup media trust rebounded to 33 percent in a 2026 release. One-year move, not a reversal, until confirmed by a second reading.
12. **Food insecurity after 2024.** The US Department of Agriculture announced in September 2025 that it would discontinue the annual food-security report after 2024. No official 2025 reading will exist on the same series.
13. **"90 percent of online content synthetic by 2026."** The Europol Innovation Lab (2022) claim is widely repeated and never measured. Graphite's 2025-2026 plateau (about 50 percent of new articles, 86 percent of Google results human-written) contradicts it for text. Do not use the Europol figure as a fact.
14. **Deezer stream share.** The 0.5 percent figure was the September 2025 reading; later 2026 coverage reports 1-3 percent. Use the dated figure, not a single number.
15. **Student-debt totals.** The 1.64 trillion dollar figure (second quarter 2025) and the about 1.7 trillion dollar federal-portfolio figure (2026 reporting) come from different bases. Name the basis with the number.
